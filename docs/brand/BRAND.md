# Second Half — Brand Book, Vol. 1

*Working document. Everything the brand touches — Instagram, website, packaging, the future shop — is derived from this file.*

---

## 1. The idea

Somebody wore this shirt to the match of their life. To the night they'll never forget. That's where the marks came from — and that's why they matter.

**Second Half sells real vintage football shirts, really worn, honestly priced around €100.** Not mint pieces for glass cases. Not factory fakes with no past. Shirts with a first half behind them — looking for someone to write the second.

**Tagline:** *The second half is yours.*

## 2. Positioning

| | |
|---|---|
| **For** | People (roughly 18–35) who love football culture and style — blokecore natives, casual collectors — who want a real shirt, not a €400 grail and not a fake |
| **We are** | The place where worn shirts find their next story |
| **Unlike** | Archive resellers (museum energy, premium prices) and replica/fake sellers (no past, no truth) |
| **Because** | Every mark is a memory, and honesty about them is our signature |

**The two enemies (never imitate, always contrast):**
1. **The glass case** — mint-condition worship, €400 price tags, shirts as investments.
2. **The factory fake** — retro reprints with no history.

**The gap we own:** competitors (Classic Football Shirts, Cult Kits) tell *football's* stories — famous matches, legendary kits. Second Half tells *people's* stories — the one who wore it, the one who'll wear it next.

## 3. Manifesto

1. **Worn, not worn out.**
2. **Every mark is a memory.**
3. **No glass cases. Ever.**

## 4. Verbal identity

**Name:** Second Half. A shirt's career doesn't end — it gets a second half, and it's yours.

**The lexicon** (use these words consistently; they build recognition):

| Ours | Never |
|---|---|
| The Rack (the shop/collection) | "catalog", "inventory" |
| The Marks (condition, listed openly) | "defects", "flaws", "90% condition" |
| First Half (the shirt's past) | "provenance" (too museum) |
| Second Half (your ownership) | "purchase" |
| Start its second half (buy CTA) | "Add to cart", "Buy now" |
| Wearers (customers, community) | "buyers", "collectors" |
| Drop (a new small collection) | "new arrivals" |
| Stories from the Wearers (the films) | "testimonials", "reviews" |

**Voice rules:**
- Write like film subtitles: short, concrete, warm. Second person.
- Always honest about condition; never apologize for it. The marks are listed *before* the shirt is praised: "Read the marks before you fall in love."
- Stories may be known or imagined — imagined ones are written so it's clear ("The fade says summers of five-a-side. The rest is yours to write."). Never fabricate provenance as fact.
- Football-native, never gatekeeping. No "premium", no "authentic vintage!!", no "rare investment piece", no SALE shouting.

## 5. Visual identity — Palette A "Sunday League"

*Rule one: the brand wears no club's colours. The shirts bring the colour — the brand is the paper their stories are printed on.* (Decided in Germany, where club-colour rivalries are real: no black+yellow (BVB), no royal blue (Schalke), no red+white pairing. Cream + pitch green belongs to no club.)

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#F3EDDF` | The ground — warm cream, every surface |
| Espresso | `#2E2419` | The words — all primary text |
| Pitch | `#35573B` | **The recognition colour** — one green, everywhere, always pointing at something that matters |
| Sepia | `#8C7B63` | The whisper — secondary text |
| Card | `#FAF7EF` | Surfaces — cards, tiles |

Warm and light on purpose: dark palettes read premium/distant; Second Half reads like a door that's open. (Photography may still be nocturnal — night shots pop on cream.)

**Logo:** "Second Half" wordmark in Fraunces Black, "Half" in Pitch green. Monogram: **the halftime clock** — a circle split vertically, left half espresso outline (the first half, played), right half solid pitch green (the second half, yours). Must read at favicon size.

**Typography — the two voices:**
- **The storyteller:** Fraunces (display; *italic* for story lines — they read like film subtitles / memoir).
- **The archivist:** Space Mono (labels, prices, the Marks — the truth-telling voice).
- Utility: Archivo (body text, quiet, everywhere else).
- Every brand surface speaks twice: the storyteller falls in love, the archivist tells the truth. Never one without the other.

**The signature device — mark annotations:** every shirt image carries its marks annotated in pitch green: a small "+" on the spot, a thin line, a mono label ("+ pulled thread, left cuff"). On the site, on Instagram, on the hang tag. *We point at what others hide.* This is the recognize-at-a-glance element.

**Layout rules:** no border-radius, hairline rules, generous space, editorial scale contrast (huge display vs small mono labels).

## 6. Photography direction

People wear the shirts. Never flat-lays, never mannequins, never studio-white.

1. **Golden Hour** — 35mm warmth after five-a-side; real streets, real light.
2. **Night** — flash-lit, 1am, in the city. Motion allowed, blur welcome.
3. **Close** — macro on the marks; the fabric is the landscape.

## 7. Instagram

**The feed is the film, not the shop window.**

Content pillars:
1. **Stories from the Wearers** (hero format) — cinematic 60–90s films: a person, their shirt, their story. Multiple angles, story-driven edit, subtitle styling in brand type. Not talking-head interviews.
2. **The Marks** — macro carousels of one shirt's marks, annotated in pitch green; last slide reveals the shirt.
3. **Halftime Talks** — manifesto/football-culture text cards in brand type.
4. **Selling happens in Stories & Highlights only. Never the feed.** (Highlight: "The Rack".)

Bio: `SECOND HALF — Worn, not worn out. Real vintage shirts, real stories. ~€100. One of each. The rack ↓`

## 8. Reference brands (what we learned)

- **Patagonia Worn Wear**: stories of worn gear build cult loyalty; "worn = loved" works commercially.
- **MUNDIAL magazine**: football nostalgia + editorial warmth creates paying community.
- **Blokecore trend**: the demand tailwind — Gen Z wears vintage shirts as fashion and wants the real thing.
- **Cult Kits / Classic Football Shirts**: competitors professionalizing; their gap is intimacy — they sell football's history, we tell people's stories.
